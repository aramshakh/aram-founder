Projects

Eight things I have built since I was twenty-one. Three made money, one is running now, the rest are closed. Each one is here with what actually happened to it, because a list of launches without endings tells you nothing.

2026 to nowDecato

Interior design workflow software, built with Vladimir Borisov. You hand it a floor plan and a brief. It lays out an editable room, sources products that exist and fit, and assembles something a client can approve and a supplier can quote.

Around twenty designers have tried it and three pay for it, with sessions running past three hours. Small numbers, but real ones.

It is narrower than anything I built before, on purpose. Empy failed because I chose a mission over a problem, so this time the wedge is a single check somebody performs every day: does this product actually fit this room, at this price.

2026 to nowHermes

My own agent, running on a five-euro server I rent. It holds notes across months, runs the recurring work on a schedule, and I reach it from Telegram. Nobody sold it to me and it has one user.

In 2026 I ran workshops in Yerevan where thirty people brought a laptop and had their own agent running by the end of the afternoon. Five mentors volunteered, unpaid.

It is the least impressive thing on this page and the one I use most. Everything else here was built to be sold; this was built because I needed it, which is probably the lesson.

2021 to 2026Empy.ai

It started as research: a transformer fine-tuned to tell an evaluation apart from an observation in written speech, at 82% F1. Then a Slack bot that caught conflict in team chat. Then a macOS app that sat in a call and coached you through it in real time.

Four thousand users. A $165,000 pre-seed. 43 companies through pilots and subscriptions. Second product of the day on Product Hunt. A Burning Heroes final in San Francisco and a live pitch at Plug and Play Silicon Valley. US pilots with Erickson Coaching International and Facility Solutions Group. Draper University, then Plug and Play Armenia.

The Electron build crashed often enough to lose every user it gained, so in February 2026 I locked myself in a room for three weeks and rewrote it in Swift. That fixed the crashes and did not fix the business. I closed it in May 2026.

What it taught me is on the about page and at more length in this post: I was driven by a mission rather than a problem, and a mission excuses the absence of feedback for years at a time.

2022 to 2023Retreat Zone

An aggregator for retreats. The idea was that people who most need to disconnect are the worst at booking it: I wanted to help someone pick their first retreat, check it actually fit what they needed, and handle the transfers and accommodation around it.

I raised my first investor check on it. It did not work, and I stopped.

It was the first time I built for a market I was inside rather than one I had read about, and the first time raising money did not turn into traction. Both lessons arrived again, larger, with Empy.

2020 to 2025Communication training

Not a product. A practice: fourteen retreats and one-on-one work for founders and executives on how they actually talk to each other, and over three hundred people trained in nonviolent communication.

In one six-month stretch it did $70,000 in revenue. Across the same years, it earned roughly five times what the startup did.

Empy was an attempt to put this into software. That did not work, but it is still the clearest thing I know how to do, and it is why the interesting problem in Decato is a constraint check rather than a picture.

before thatThe first three

An ad agency, at twenty-one. Over $4,000 a month, then bankrupt inside a year.

A first startup, at twenty-three. Bankrupt again. I took two full-time jobs at twenty-four to clear the debt.

A real estate product, at twenty-eight. Built it and sold it within three weeks, the same year my mother died.

I list these because the pattern only reads correctly at full length. Anyone can have one failure explained away; eight attempts with the endings named is a different claim.
