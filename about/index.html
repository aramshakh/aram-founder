About

I build software that holds the rules of a messy situation. Right now that means rooms. Decato takes a floor plan and a brief, lays out an editable room, sources real products that actually fit, and assembles a client-ready deck. The interesting part is not the render. It is that everything in the room can be bought, and that it fits.

Before Decato I spent four years on Empy.ai. It started as a Slack bot that caught conflict in team chat and became communication intelligence for high-stakes calls. We worked with 43 companies across pilots and subscriptions, raised a $165K pre-seed, ran US pilots with Erickson Coaching International and Facility Solutions Group, and hit second product of the day on Product Hunt. I closed it in 2026. Decato is the next attempt on a much narrower wedge.

Before that I was an analytical consultant at Google from 2020 to 2021, on accounts including Semrush, Miro, Veeam and Wrike across the US, Europe and Asia. Earlier still: product management at inKin, marketing lead at Cryptology in Singapore, and performance work at Krisp, Artec 3D and adidas.

Alongside the building I advise. I spent 2025 with Pink Elephant, an AI and GTM fund, on deal evaluation and positioning, and I work with Armenian Code Academy on how founders and operators actually adopt AI.

The thread runs further back than any of the companies. At eighteen I ran a small digital agency, building websites and marketing for whoever would pay, while I was still at school. Before that I was building Warcraft III custom maps: you take an engine someone else wrote and build a situation inside it with its own physics, then let other people live in there. Empy did that for conversations. Decato does it for rooms. It has always been the same work.

The step between the two is easy to miss. While I was at Google I took a master's in psychoanalysis at the Higher School of Economics, and finished it in 2021. A year later I was training a model to tell an evaluation apart from an observation in someone's speech. Learning to read a person as a system of rules and then trying to write that system down are the same instinct, one after the other.

What I am drawn to are products that carry someone all the way to output they can ship, rather than stopping at a demo that impresses and leaves the real work untouched.

I build Decato with my co-founder Vladimir Borisov. I live in Yerevan, and the fastest way to reach me is email.
