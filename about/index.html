About

I build software that holds the rules of a messy situation. Right now that means rooms. Decato takes a floor plan and a brief, lays out an editable room, sources real products that actually fit, and assembles a client-ready deck. The interesting part is not the render. It is that everything in the room can be bought, and that it fits.

Before Decato I spent four years on Empy.ai. It started as a Slack bot that caught conflict in team chat and became communication intelligence for high-stakes calls. We reached 4,000 users, raised a $165K pre-seed, went through Draper University and Plug and Play Armenia, and hit second product of the day on Product Hunt. I closed it in 2026. Decato is the next attempt on a much narrower wedge.

Before that I was an analytical consultant at Google from 2020 to 2021, working with large B2B SaaS and fintech clients across the US, EU and APAC. Earlier still: product management at inKin, marketing lead at Cryptology in Singapore, and performance work at Krisp, Artec 3D and adidas.

The thread runs further back than any of the companies. I grew up building Warcraft III custom maps: you take an engine someone else wrote and build a situation inside it with its own physics, then let other people live in there. Empy did that for conversations. Decato does it for rooms. It has always been the same work.

I also teach it. In 2026 I ran workshops in Yerevan where people brought a laptop, bought a five-euro server, and had their first autonomous agent running by the end of the afternoon. Thirty people came and five mentors volunteered, unpaid. The agent that runs my own work came out of the same habit.

What I am drawn to are products that carry someone all the way to output they can ship, rather than stopping at a demo that impresses and leaves the real work untouched.

I build Decato with my co-founder Vladimir Borisov. I live in Yerevan, and the fastest way to reach me is email.
