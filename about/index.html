My story

Between 2022 and 2026 I tried twelve times to ship a product. I went through eleven co-founders. I did about two hundred investor calls over Zoom. I spent $40,000 of my own money, which was everything I had saved at Google, and $155,000 of other people's. Over three years the company earned under $15,000. I finished with $11,000 of debt and three accelerator programmes that were worth nothing.

I can tell you what each of those bought. That is the only reason to list them.

For most of that time I thought the problem was execution. It wasn't. I was building a mission instead of a product. A mission lives in the future, so it can never be shown to be wrong. When there was no revenue I said I wasn't in it for the money. When there were no users I said the market wasn't ready. Every month I found an intelligent reason the result hadn't arrived yet. We sold air and called it the future, and we believed it.

The company was Empy: conversation AI for high-stakes calls. It was not nothing. Four thousand people used it. Forty-three companies came through pilots and subscriptions. It was second product of the day on Product Hunt, went through Draper University and Plug and Play, and was one of nine finalists at Burning Heroes in San Francisco. In February 2026 I rewrote the client in Swift. That fixed the crashes and not the business, and I closed the company in May.

The whole time I was running a business that worked, and I kept forgetting to count it. Four-day residential communication programmes for senior managers. Fourteen of them. More than three hundred people trained. $1,700 a head, $70,000 in one six-month stretch. It earned about five times what the software did.

I treated it as a side effect, because it didn't look like the thing I wanted to be. That is a bad reason, and it cost me most of a decade.

I was born in Moscow. My parents had left Armenia in the nineties, the way people left then, which is to say because of hunger. My father worked, and eventually had a shopping centre and a row of market stalls. My first words were Armenian and I barely spoke Russian before school. Russian is still the language in which I feel I have to earn the right to talk.

I spent most of my life assuming I would be the first person in my family to do anything difficult. At thirty I found out about a cousin who left for America at eighteen, cleaned a petrol station, and is a professor now. And about a great-uncle who took an engineering degree in Iran in the sixties and built the infrastructure of the north of the country. I was not the first. I was late.

Here is the part I am still working on. I learned early, in school, that you do not have to take the direct route. You can be clever, be liked, negotiate, do the minimum and not fail. It worked, so it stopped being a tactic and became the way I live. Anything that required doing one thing at a time and being answerable for the result frightened me out of all proportion.

I have almost no memory of myself between eleven and seventeen. What I remember is games. In a game the goal is stated, the rules hold, and things end. It was the only place where finishing was not dangerous. I wrote custom maps for Warcraft III, which is not playing: you write the rules and other people live inside them.

Everything I have built since is a system of rules for a messy human situation. That sounds like a calling and it is also the shape of the problem. I am better at seeing than at doing. I have been correcting for that since December 2025, deliberately, with a list.

The seeing part is not abstract. At a retreat in Armenia in 2022 one man in the group could not be in contact: he interrupted, he fidgeted, he pulled the room apart, and everyone had written him off. We talked for a few hours and I showed him how he sounded and where he left. The group changed that evening and so did he. That is the thing I can do reliably. It does not scale, which is most of my problem.

Before any of it I spent ten years on other people's budgets — adidas, Artec 3D, a product marketing team in Singapore, Krisp, inKin, then two years as an analytical consultant at Google. I was good at other people's growth long before I was any good at my own.

I also started a master's in psychoanalysis at the Higher School of Economics in 2021, which ran alongside training a model to tell evaluation from observation in speech. I did not plan that. They turned out to be the same problem.

Now I build Decato with Vladimir Borisov. A floor plan goes in and a room a designer can actually order comes out: dimensioned, priced, assembled from furniture that exists. It is a narrow problem after a very wide one, which is the point.

Fifty people have used it. The first session averages six hours. One customer in the US pays for it. Those numbers are small and I intend to keep publishing them.
