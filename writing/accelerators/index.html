Why 99% of accelerators outside the US waste your time

May 1, 2025 · translated from Russian

They do not invest. They administrate.

Picture it: a partner from Antler in Singapore writes to you.
Invites you to apply.
The link goes to a landing page with a buggy AI bot that asks for everything in one question: your home address, the whole cap table, documents, traction, very nearly your DNA.
No feedback. No conversation. Just fill in the form and maybe we notice you.

You do not know who reads it.
You do not know who decides.
You do not even know whether they have the authority to decide anything at all.

And then, if you do make it to a call, you are interviewed by a partner from Saudi Arabia who has been through fewer startups than you have.
Half the time he does not follow the product, the other half he talks nonsense.
After the call you do not want to update your pitch deck. You want to tell them where to go.

Where is the problem?
Antler, and they are not alone, are scaling a brand, not expertise.
It is a franchise.
The local partners are not investors. They are operations managers with a slide deck.

Markets outside the US give you mentorship at best.
At worst they take your founder resource and then decline to make any decision at all.

Real accelerators are not forms.
They are people who invest in you before the deal.
You feel it immediately.
That is YC, Techstars, 500 Startups, Plug and Play, and a few rare places in London, the Netherlands and Barcelona.

If you are building something alive, you do not need an administrative filter.
You need a person who understands who you are and what you are building.
Otherwise this is not acceleration. It is an HR procedure.
