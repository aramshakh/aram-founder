What Draper actually taught me

December 4, 2025

I came to Draper University as a “builder.”
Left realizing I’d been building in a vacuum, not in reality.

I thought I understood Silicon Valley.

It took me two days to realize I didn’t.
San Francisco hit different.

I arrived thinking the city would feel like home for ambitious founders. It didn’t.
Just thousands of people trying to survive, same as me.

At Draper, most of the important things weren’t part of the official program.

One of the best conversations happened on a day I skipped a lecture. I met Ilyaz, a founder from Kyrgyzstan building artificial skin for prosthetics.
We walked and talked for hours.
That conversation taught me more than the class we missed.
The real curriculum was often the person sitting next to you, not the scheduled speaker.

There were people I didn’t click with - some came to network and have fun, others weren’t building anything serious.
Earlier, I would’ve dismissed them outright. Instead, I tried to stay open. Draper showed me how limited my perspective was and how hard it is to stay flexible when your instinct is to judge.

I thought I was open-minded before. I wasn’t.

Survival Week sharpened everything.
Not the official “leadership training”, but the moments stripped from comfort.
That’s where friendships formed: with Fawaz, with Aigerim, with Ilyaz.
Shared difficulty creates a different kind of bond.
Stronger than the ones you make at parties or demo days.

The most underrated part of Draper wasn’t the program.
It was living together.
Sharing a house, routines, dishes, small chaos.
Only later I realized how valuable that was.
Those connections stayed real after the lectures faded.

The most overrated part?
Probably the content I already knew.
I arrived with experience, and some parts felt too basic.
But the human parts - the cultural differences, pitching under pressure, forced adaptation during Survival Week - mattered.

The biggest shift was internal.

I came thinking I was “better” or “further along” than many. I wasn’t.
It was humbling.

Around the middle of the program, I realized a lot of what I’d been building was fantasy. The product roadmap, the partnership pipeline, the markets I convinced myself were ready - all of it lived more in my head than in reality.
Draper didn’t break that illusion, but being around people who were executing made it impossible to ignore anymore.

What you don’t see in photos are the things that mattered:
late-night rooftop conversations, singing with people who felt like strangers a week ago, talking honestly about the parts of founder life that don’t make it into social posts - fear, paralysis, doubt.

If I had to describe Draper to myself a year ago:
“Every dream has a meeting with reality. And that meeting is not a disappointment, but a beginning.”

I played the San Francisco game for a bit.
The dream version.
Then it faded, and what remained was simple, solid, real.
And that’s the part that stays with me.
