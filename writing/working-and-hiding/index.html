Working hard, and hiding

May 17, 2026

There's a kind of working hard that's almost indistinguishable from hiding.

For the last few years, I was living in a strange in-between state.

From the outside, it looked like movement: working, building, taking calls, trying again.

But internally, a lot of it was not building.
It was survival disguised as building.

After eight moves in twelve months and years of constant pressure, I was much more overloaded than I wanted to admit.

The hardest part wasn't that things didn't work.

It was admitting what was actually true:

I was not “almost there.”

I was not “one final push away.”

That story gave me hope, but it also kept me trapped in the same loop:

just a little longer,
just one more push,
then things will finally stabilize.

But “then” never came.

And when “then” never comes, you have to ask a harder question:

What if the model itself is wrong?

I had spent years trying to break through with a way of living and working that my system could no longer handle.

Too much pressure.
Too much proving.
Too much future-based identity.
Too little contact with where I actually was.

As long as you believe you are “almost breaking through,” you don’t change the model.
You just press harder on the same overheated system.

Pressing harder can feel like discipline.

Like ambition. Like resilience.

But sometimes it is just avoidance with better branding.

At some point, reality stops negotiating.

It shows up through the body.
Through money.
Through the team.
Through relationships.

The shift started when I stopped asking:

“How do I push harder?”

And started asking:

What is actually true right now?

I had been living in survival mode for a long time, while still trying to build as if I was operating from stability.

That sentence changed something for me.

It was more practical than strong vs. weak, failure vs. genius.

The previous way of living and working was no longer adequate to my actual state.

And once I saw that, I could stop building from a false point.

I don’t have a clean success story to wrap this in.

I don’t know exactly where this leads.

But for the first time in a long time, something in me has released.

Like a kettle that had been under too much pressure and finally let the steam out.

There is less tension now.

Which means I can think again. Plan again.

Make decisions from reality, not from the internal myth of who I was “almost becoming.”

For me, this feels like a very adult shift:

to stop confusing survival mode with acceleration,
to stop calling pressure a strategy,
and to start building from what is actually true:

how much energy I really have,
what pace I can actually sustain,
what kind of business model fits my life,
what kind of team I really need,
and what decisions I have been postponing for too long.

Maybe that is what business failure is supposed to teach.

Not just how to win next time.

But how to stop lying to yourself about where you are starting from.
